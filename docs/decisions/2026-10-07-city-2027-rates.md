# 2026-10-07: City of Mesa recommended rates for February 2027

## Decision

Jennifer shared two City documents from Legistar file 26-0773 (mesaaz.gov, Management and Budget, Presentations and Reports):

* The FY 26/27 Utility Rates Recommendation presentation (62 pages).
* Attachment 2, Utility Fund Cash Flow Projections (5 pages).

Both are transcribed in `sources/city/`. The presentation gives the landscape rates, so it is the source for 2027. Added `data/rates/2027-02-01.md` with `status: recommended`:

* Usage $5.95 + 15% = $6.84 (Non-residential Landscape, page 12).
* Excess surcharge in two tiers: $3.44 from 100% to 150% of the winter water average and $3.74 above 150% (pages 12 to 14). These are blocks of $10.28 and $10.58 including usage.
* Drought charge $0.13 (page 11).
* Service charge +3.5%, the AF&E recommendation (page 11): $56.94 for the 1 1/2 inch meters and $40.68 for the 1 inch meters. Staff recommended +4.0%.
* Superfund charge, the environmental fee, and taxes are not in the presentation and stay at today's amounts.
* Effective February 1, 2027 (page 61). The Council votes December 8, 2026.

## Why landscape and not "commercial"

The HOA is a commercial customer, in the City's Commercial Landscape class. The presentation's typical "Commercial - Landscape" bill (page 19) is the HOA's own prices to the cent: $55.01 + 30 x $5.95 + 30 x $0.08 = $235.91 today, and $56.94 + 30 x $6.84 + 30 x $0.13 = $266.04 under AF&E. Tests check both. The excess surcharge above the winter average is also the landscape surcharge. General commercial would be +13% usage and +13% and +23% surcharge tiers, which is noted in the rate file's todo in case the City bills the HOA as general commercial.

The cash-flow sheet's "WATER Commercial (usage)" row (+13% FY 26/27, +13% FY 27/28, then +5%) is general commercial. It is kept in `sources/` for reference and is not used to price anything. Its FY 25/26 +20% does equal the HOA's February 2026 usage price change ($4.76 to $5.71).

## How the site uses it

* A recommended rate never prices a past bill and is never "today's rate". `currentRate()` skips it, and the model exposes it separately as `nextRate`. `data/rates/2026-05-13.md` now ends at read periods through 2027-01-31.
* Quick wins and What if can each be priced at "today's City prices" or "the City's recommended prices from February 2027". Later years are held at 2027 prices (site default).
* Jennifer's Zone Plan Model used the same proposal, so the workbook's 2027 numbers are confirmed. The plan file no longer carries its own copy of the prices.
